## results that differ from expectation

- a: silencing TD units hurts performance no more than silencing Int units or random choice units, in every category and in either task of the changed-response pairs (anti task: −TD −0.14 vs −Int −0.18; yes/no task: −0.07 vs −0.19), and no more on the other 25 tasks (−0.073 vs −0.071). TD units are not needed for behaviour in these networks. Silencing TI units hurts most everywhere except the anti task of reversed pairs, where all four sets do equal damage.
- b: the XOR cost of removing TD units is carried by the same- and different-family pairs (−0.015 / −0.018, p = 0.002), where XOR is not a stimulus variable; it is small and not significant in spatial vs abstract (p = 0.064) and small in reversed-mapping pairs (p = 0.027).
- c: TD subsets carry more task identity than TI subsets before go (0.65 vs 0.60, p = 0.002) but not after go (p = 0.064), and not in spatial vs abstract pairs.
- d: in reversed-mapping pairs TD units are stimulus-side coders. This makes them an imperfect analog of FEF TD units, which reverse although target and movement direction agree in both tasks.
